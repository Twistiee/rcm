# rcm enclosure

A compact, 3D-printable housing for the rcm board: an open-top tray plus a lid.
Two lids are generated — the **keypad** face carrying eight 25 mm anti-vandal
switches, and a **blank** plate that is the starting point for the
relay-control lid (whose I/O leaves through the lid rather than the box).
The keypad build also gets a small **guard ring** that bonds over the
start/stop button.

Everything is generated by one script. There is nothing hand-modelled, so any
dimension can be changed and the parts rebuilt in about a minute.

```
"C:/Program Files/FreeCAD 1.1/bin/freecadcmd.exe" build_enclosure.py              # both revisions
"C:/Program Files/FreeCAD 1.1/bin/freecadcmd.exe" build_enclosure.py revB-8way    # just one
"C:/Program Files/FreeCAD 1.1/bin/freecadcmd.exe" render_views.py revB-8way       # review images
```

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `build_enclosure.py` | the model. `PARAMS` and `VARIANTS` at the top are the only blocks to edit |
| `render_views.py` | shaded views, sections and the bezel print profile into `images/`, no GUI needed |
| `*-revA.FCStd`, `*-revB-8way.FCStd` | **generated — never edit these by hand**, they are overwritten every build |
| `exports/*.3mf` | **for the slicer** — opens straight into PrusaSlicer / OrcaSlicer, in mm |
| `exports/*.step` | for other CAD |
| `images/*-revA.png`, `images/*-revB-8way.png` | review renders, one set per revision |

## Two revisions, and which one you want

Both are built, both are kept, because both are real.

| | revA | **revB-8way** |
|---|---|---|
| Wire entry | 2-way PWR one end, 4-way CAN the other | **one 8-way, one end** |
| For | the relay-module build | **the keypad build** |
| Outside | 162 × 102 × 51.5 | 162 × 102 × 51.5 |
| Box height set by | the switch stack | the switch stack |
| Short-wall screw span | 92 mm | **46 mm** |
| Harness headroom | 30 needed, 32.7 there | 30 needed, 32.7 there |

**revB-8way is the keypad build.** Eight wires arrive on one connector at one
end, which is what the keypad needs and is simply less to make up than two
looms at opposite ends. Two things fall out of that for free:

- the **+X wall is now blank**, so it can finally take a mid-span lid screw.
  That halves the 92 mm screw span, the longest run of unsupported lid;
- the 8-way flange is 35.5 mm tall against the small shell's 31.75, but since
  the lid thinned to 3.5 mm the switch stack is the taller constraint on both
  revisions, so they are now the same height.

Nothing about the lids, the switches or the guard ring differs between them.

## Which way round it goes in the car

The keypad build mounts behind the gearstick, to the driver's left, with the
board **longways** — a short side facing the front of the car. The buttons
therefore run **4 deep front-to-rear and 2 across**.

Which short side faces forward is `car_front`, and it is **−X**, because the
loom comes from the front of the car and enters through the DEUTSCH port,
which is on the `-X` wall.

| | |
|---|---|
| **−X** | front of the car (`car_front`) |
| **+Z** | up |
| **+Y** | the car's **right** — so on a RHD car the **driver's side is +Y** |

Forward and up fix everything else: in a right-handed frame the car's left is
+Y times the front sign, so with the front at −X the car's right is +Y.

**Do not hand-write one of these signs.** Ask `car_axes()`, or say which
button you mean in car terms — `("front", "driver")` — and let
`guard_switch_xy()` map it. Getting front and rear the wrong way round is
what once put the starter on the diagonally opposite corner to the one
intended, and the geometry looked perfectly reasonable the whole time. The
build now prints every port wall in car terms (`-X wall = front`), and
`images/guard_detail-*.png` draws the panel with the car directions labelled
— the quickest way to check you got the corner you meant before printing.

## Sizes

| | mm |
|---|---|
| Outside | **162 × 102 × 51.5** (box 48 + lid 3.5) |
| Cavity | 158 × 98 × 45 deep |
| Wall / floor | **2** / 3 |
| PCB | 140 × 80 as delivered, on 5 mm standoffs, 9 mm clear to the walls |

**The board is 140 × 80, not 140 × 70.** The routed outline in
`rcm.kicad_pcb` really is 140 × 70 — that is what Edge.Cuts says — but the
boards as delivered carry a manufacturing rail on each long edge, because the
components sit close enough to the edge that the panel needs something to hold
on to. Those rails are being **kept on and used as a labelling surface**,
since the silkscreen does not label the terminals, so the box has to house
the 80 mm envelope.

The model keeps the two ideas apart and you should too:

- `pcb_w` / `pcb_d` are the **routed outline**, and `pcb_holes` are measured
  against it in genuine KiCad board coordinates;
- `pcb_tab_w` is the rail, and it only widens the **envelope**.

Set `pcb_tab_w` to 0 if you ever snap them off and the box shrinks by 10 mm
without anything else moving. Had the 80 mm simply been written into `pcb_d`,
the mapping in `board_to_global()` would have shifted every standoff 5 mm and
the board would not have fitted its own mounting holes.

The 9 mm gap around that envelope is not padding: it is what lets a Ø8
lid-screw boss stand in each cavity corner without touching the board, it is
and the channel the harness climbs in. Shrink it and the screws or the wires
have to go somewhere else.

The **wall is 2 mm** and the floor 3 mm. The wall used to be 5 mm, entirely so
its top could carry a seal groove; with the seal gone there is nothing asking
it to be thick, and dropping it took the outside profile in by 6 mm on each
axis and the box from 186 cm³ to 111 cm³.

## Closing it — and why there is no seal any more

**There is no O-ring seal.** `lid_seal` is `False`. The lid lands flat on the
2 mm wall top, located by its lip and pulled down by the screws. Call it
splash-resistant; it is not weatherproof.

It used to be a face seal — a 2.4 × 1.5 mm groove in the lid underside over a
2 mm silicone cord — and that is what the 5 mm wall was for. Taking the wall
to 2 mm killed it outright:

| | |
|---|---|
| Groove width | 2.4 mm |
| Wall it has to sit on | 2.0 mm |
| Land left each side | **−0.20 mm** |
| Lid screw vs groove | **−0.40 mm** (the countersink cut into it) |

The alternatives were a 1 mm cord on a 0.4 mm land, or thickening the wall top
from the inside with a ribbed step. Neither was worth it for this build, so the
groove is gone.

**To put it back** you need a wall that can carry it: set `lid_seal` to `True`
*and* `wall` to at least `seal_groove_w + 2.6` (so 5 mm for the 2 mm cord).
`checks()` will tell you, with numbers, if you have not — the groove geometry
and the cord parameters are all still there, just switched off.

Six or seven M3 screws pull it together, into brass heat-set inserts in the
bosses (Ø4.2 holes; change `boss_hole_d` to 2.6 if you would rather self-tap).
The short walls get no mid-span screw where a DEUTSCH port lives, because a
full-height boss would sit inside the connector body. That leaves 76 mm
between screws on the long walls and 92 mm on the short ones.

**revB halves the short one.** With only one DEUTSCH port, the `+X` wall is
blank and takes a mid-span screw, so that span drops 92 → 46 mm.
`screw_mid_short` accepts a list of walls for exactly this. revA has ports at
both ends and still spans 92; add positions to `screw_extra` and reprint the
lid if the lid lifts, and nothing else has to change.

There is **no USB window** — it is a service port, so take the lid off to
reach it.

## Mounting it to the car

Provisional and deliberately dumb: four Ø4.5 holes straight through the floor
at (±55, ±44.5), for M4. They sit in the 9 mm border strip that runs outside
the PCB, so the heads are reachable with a long ball-end key **without lifting
the board out**, and nothing on the board is above them.

They used to be at ±39.5, which is now **underneath the board** — the rails
reach ±40. The build checks each hole against the board *envelope*, so it
catches that; it caught exactly this when `pcb_tab_w` went in.

Each one is a hole straight through the floor. A bonded seal ("dowty") washer
under each head costs nothing and keeps road spray out of them. A button head sits proud in the strip and fouls
nothing, which is why there is no counterbore; set `floor_hole_cbore_d` if you
want one. Move or add positions in `floor_holes` — the build checks each one
against the PCB footprint, the walls and the screw bosses.

This is a placeholder for a real bracket once the seating position is settled
and it is known where the box can actually live.

## Wire entry — DEUTSCH DT, one footprint for all three sizes

Power and CAN come in through DT flange receptacles ("DT-F") in the end walls,
not through slots. That keeps the wall free of open holes and it keeps
the board's own screw terminals inside, on short pigtails.

The three sizes are interchangeable in this housing, which is worth knowing
because it means one printed part accepts whichever you have in the drawer.
From the TE customer drawings, all of **DT04-2P / -3P / -4P -L012** share the
flange (40.51 × 31.75 × 3.18) and the same four mounting holes on
**30.35 × 22.45** centres — but their recommended cutouts differ:

| | recommended cutout |
|---|---|
| 2 way | 18.42 × 18.29, R5.08 |
| 3 way | round, Ø25.4 |
| 4 way | 22.10 × 20.98, R6.35 |

A **round Ø25.4** hole contains the 4-way's rounded rectangle with 0.09 mm to
spare, so the model cuts one round aperture (Ø25.8 with fit clearance) and all
three drop in. The flange covers it in every case.

### The 8-way — revB, and three numbers that are not confirmed

The keypad build carries eight wires, so it gets one **DT04-08PA-L012** at one
end instead. Connector sizes live in `DT_FOOTPRINTS` and a port names one, so
switching is a one-word edit and the two sizes cannot get their dimensions
crossed.

**Confirmed** from TE's published data:

| | |
|---|---|
| Flange | 54.99 × 35.51 |
| Overall length | 45.67 |
| Contacts | size 16, 9.09 pitch, 4.45 row-to-row |

That mapping was *checked, not assumed*: the same fields on TE's page for the
`DT04-3P-L012` read 40.51 × 31.75, which is exactly the flange this model has
used since day one.

**Inferred, and this is why `verified` is `False`.** TE's customer drawing is
behind a login, so three numbers came from applying the small shell's own
proportions to the bigger flange:

| | | from |
|---|---|---|
| `hole_dx` | 44.83 | 5.08 mm (0.200″) inset per side, as on the 2-4 way |
| `hole_dy` | 26.21 | 4.65 mm inset per side, as on the 2-4 way |
| `aperture` | 38 × 24 R5 | sized to clear the body and keep 3.2 mm of wall to the mounting holes |
| `body_in` | 24.7 | 45.67 − flange 3.18 − the 3-way's 17.85 front end |

**Measure these before printing a box.** They are two minutes with calipers on
the connector and a ruined print if wrong. The build says so on every run, and
`checks()` refuses to be quiet about it until `verified` goes `True`.

The aperture is a rounded **rectangle**, not a round hole — the 8-way's pin
field alone is 27.3 mm wide, so a round hole big enough to pass the body would
be wider than the flange is tall. The first attempt at 40 × 26 left only
1.8 mm of wall between the aperture and the mounting holes; 38 × 24 leaves
3.2, and there is now a check that works that out for any aperture shape
rather than leaving it to be noticed in a render.

### Print `Port-Coupon-dt-8way-revB-8way` first

It is a 67 × 48 patch of the port wall — same 2 mm wall, same 3 mm inner pad,
same aperture, same four holes and nut pockets — so you can test-fit the
connector for 15 cm³ instead of finding out inside a 186 cm³ box. The build
makes one automatically for every unverified footprint, and stops making it
the moment you set `verified` to `True`.

Each port also gets:

- a 3 mm pad on the inside face, so the wall is locally 8 mm — stiffer, and it
  gives the nut pockets a solid floor;
- four Ø4.4 clearance holes (M4, or #8) with hex nut pockets on the inside, so
  the screws have something to bite without loose hardware. Set
  `dt_nut_pockets` to `False` if you would rather use your own nuts and
  washers. Screws need to be ~10 mm to cross the 5 mm wall and pad.

Ports are placed by the `dt_ports` list — label, footprint, wall, position
along it, and height. A height of `None` means **as low as the board allows**,
and the box then grows to cover the flange; that is how revB sizes itself
around the 8-way without anybody doing the sums. The revA default puts power
on one end and CAN on the other, both at z = 28,
which keeps the connector body clear of the board: it reaches 22 mm inboard,
overhanging the end 13 mm of the PCB, and **both end strips of the board were
checked against `rcm.kicad_pcb` and hold nothing but SMD parts** (the USB-C
shell at ~3.2 mm is the tallest). That fact is recorded as `pcb_clear_strip`
and the build warns if a port is moved somewhere it stops being true.

TE sell a gasket that goes between the flange and the panel. Use it.

## The harness off the long edges

Every screw terminal on this board sits on one of the two **long** edges —
`J_CH1`/`J_CH2` on one, `IGN`/`AUX`/`CAN_A`/`CAN_B` on the other — with the
plugs turned so the wires leave the board rather than running back across it.
So the wires have to climb, in the channel between the board edge and the
cavity wall, before they run to a DEUTSCH port in an end wall.

The requirement is **30 mm of clear height above the board's bottom mounting
face** (`wire_clear_h`). That datum is deliberate: it is the face you can
actually get a rule on, unlike the board top or the cavity floor.

The box gives **32.7 mm**, so it clears by 2.7 mm:

```
board bottom face   z =  8.0     floor 3 + standoff 5
plug bodies         z = 18.6     9 mm tall, on top of the board
                    ...          the climb
switch nut, lowest  z = 40.7     <- the ceiling
lid underside       z = 48.0
```

The ceiling is the **corner of the nearest switch nut**, not the lid: at
35 mm row pitch a 32.2 AF nut spans 37.2 across corners, which reaches
|y| = 36.1, and the wires climb outboard of the plug bodies at |y| = 34.2.
`derive()` works that out rather than trusting the number above, and
`checks()` fails the build if it stops being true — so changing the row
pitch, the lid thickness or the standoffs will tell you immediately if it
has eaten the wire space. `images/sections-*.png` draws the channel in green.

It was 1.7 mm when the lid was 6 mm thick. Thinning the lid to 3.5 pushed the
switch deeper, the cavity grew to suit, and the margin came with it. If a fat
cable or a tie-wrap needs more, raise `top_clearance` and everything else
follows.

## The one number to go and measure

`pcb_tall_comp_h` = 12 mm, the tallest thing standing on the board — assumed
to be the Waveshare buck module on its header pins. **It sets the height of
the whole box on its own**, because at 35 mm switch pitch the switches sit
directly over the buck, so the switch tail and the component stack:

```
inner height = standoff 5 + PCB 1.6 + component 12 + switch tail 23.8 + air 2 = 45
```

Measure the real assembly and put the number in. Every millimetre off the
component height is a millimetre off the box.

## The switches

The switch numbers are not new work — they are lifted from
`G:\My Drive\CAD\Exocet\Keypad\Keypad-Face.FCStd`, which was printed and
fitted and worked: Ø25.0 through hole, Ø28.0 head, 35 mm pitch, 2 rows of 4.
The Ø25 hole and the pitch are untouched — do not change them without a test
print. The recess over the head is no longer that part's Ø28.0 × 2.0; see
**The lid is 3.5 mm** below for why it is now Ø28.2 × 0.5.

What did change is how the recess ends. A square shoulder from Ø28 down to
Ø25 leaves a **1.5 mm flat annular ceiling** — printed face down, that is a
horizontal overhang and the one feature on either part that needs support. It
is now a **45° cone** instead: the recess stays full Ø28.2 for its 0.5 mm,
and then the bore closes to Ø25 over the next 1.6 mm at exactly the angle a
slicer will bridge unaided. See `images/bezel_detail-*.png`, which draws the
profile the way it prints.

`sw_bezel_transition` also takes `"radius"` (a quarter-round — softer to look
at, but it runs tangent to the seat, so a narrow ring of it ends up near
horizontal and may want support after all) and `"step"` for the original.

### The lid is 3.5 mm, and the nut is why

It was 6 mm. The nut won the argument: every millimetre of lid is a millimetre
of thread the nut does not get, and 3.5 mm is the most the switch can carry.

It only fits because **`sw_bezel_depth` came down from 2.0 mm to 0.5 mm**. That
number used to be the full head thickness, so the head sat flush. Now it is
only a register: the head lands on its O-ring, the O-ring lands on the 45°
chamfer, and 0.5 mm is all the straight wall needed to locate it. The cone
still gets its 1.6 mm, which leaves 1.4 mm of straight Ø25 bore under it to
line the thread up in.

| | was | now |
|---|---|---|
| Lid thickness | 6.0 | **3.5** |
| Top-face recess | Ø28.0 × 2.0 deep | **Ø28.2 × 0.5 deep** |
| Thread left for the nut | 6.3 | **7.3** |
| Straight bore under the cone | 2.5 | 1.4 |
| Keypad lid volume | 81.5 cm³ | **47.7 cm³** |

The recess is Ø28.2, 0.2 over the Ø28 head, so it drops in rather than being
pressed in. `sw_head_d` and `sw_head_t` now describe the switch head itself,
separately from the recess that takes it — the model needs both to know how
far the head stands proud.

**The head is no longer flush. It stands 1.5 mm proud** (`sw_head_t` −
`sw_bezel_depth`), and a thick O-ring under it will lift it further. That is
what the guard ring has to clear, which is why `guard_h` went to 6.5: the
figure that matters is the **5.0 mm of guard above the button face**, and
`checks()` now enforces that against `guard_min_above_button` rather than
trusting the ring height.

Two things follow from the thinner lid and are worth knowing:

- **The switch hangs 2.5 mm lower into the box** (24.3 mm below the lid, not
  21.8), so the cavity grew to keep it off the buck module. Overall the box is
  still *shorter* — 51.5 mm outside against 52 for the old revA and 54 for
  revB — because the lid gave up more than the box took.
- **Bending stiffness is down about five-fold** (it goes as thickness cubed).
  With no seal to squeeze, that matters less than it would have; it now just
  sets how flat the lid sits on the wall top. revB's 46 mm short-wall span
  helps; revA still spans 92 mm. `screw_extra` takes more positions.

## The start/stop guard

One button — the **front one on the driver's side**, at (−52.5, 17.5) —
starts and stops the car, and it sits where a knee or a sleeve can reach it.

It is at the **front** because the loom arrives from the front of the car and
the DEUTSCH port is on that wall, so the starter is at the same end the wires
come in. `guard_sw` is `("front", "driver")` and `car_front` is `-X`; between
them the pin holes, the checks and the corner coupon all land on the right
corner without a single sign being typed by hand. It gets a raised
collar: Ø38 outside, Ø30 bore, **6.5 mm proud of the lid** — which is
**5.0 mm above the button**, since the head itself now stands 1.5 mm proud. A
fingertip drops straight in; a palm cannot.

**Both top edges are broken at 45°**, 1 mm in the bore and 1 mm on the
outside, leaving 2 mm flat on top of the 4 mm wall. This is the one part on
the whole assembly you are meant to prod at in the dark, so neither edge is
left sharp — the bore edge is what your fingertip slides down, the outer edge
is what the back of your hand catches. `guard_chamfer_in` and
`guard_chamfer_out` set them separately and the build warns if the pair of
them have eaten the wall.

The **base is deliberately left square** against the lid. A chamfer there
would only open a dirt trap round the bond line, and no finger reaches it.

**It is a separate printed part, and that is not laziness.** The lid prints
face down — that is the entire reason the bezel recess ends in a 45° cone
instead of a square shoulder. Anything standing proud of the front face
therefore points straight into the bed. Turning the lid over only moves the
problem onto the 2 mm locating lip, which would then hold the whole lid 2 mm
off the bed with nothing under it. So the ring is its own part, and the lid
keeps its cone, its lip and its face-down print unchanged.

It is located by **two Ø3 × 3 mm pins** into two blind Ø3.2 × 3.5 holes in
the lid, so it cannot be bonded off-centre or crooked and you are not trying
to eyeball concentricity while the glue goes off. The holes leave 2.5 mm of
lid under them and never break through.

The pins sit on a **45° diagonal** (`guard_pin_angle`), which is the furthest
they get from both neighbouring buttons — 10.3 mm of clearance, against
2.4 mm if they were on the X axis. The build checks every pin hole against
every switch recess and every lid screw, so move the guard to another button
and it will tell you if the pins have landed somewhere silly.

**Printing it:** bond face **up** — guard end on the bed, pins printed last.
That is the only orientation in which the pins are not overhangs, and it is
kind to both chamfers as well: printed this way the outer one widens going up
and the bore one closes in going up, both at 45°, so neither needs support
either. It is about 2.5 cm³, so a few minutes.

**Fitting it:** drop the pins in dry first and check the ring is concentric
with the button head before any glue. The Ø30 bore clears the Ø28 head by
1 mm all round, so a rocking ring means a pin is proud, not that the hole is
wrong.

## Corner coupons — the real test print

`Corner-Coupon-Lid-*` and `Corner-Coupon-Box-*` are a **matched pair**: the
corner of the keypad lid nearest the start/stop button, and the top 20 mm of
the box corner it lands on. About 56 × 61 mm, 9.3 cm³ and 12.3 cm³, so the
two together are roughly 27 g — against 12 hours for the box.

Between them they answer everything the flat coupon cannot:

| | |
|---|---|
| **The switch** | Ø25 bore, the Ø28.2 × 0.5 recess, the 45° cone, and whether the nut has thread to grab on a 3.5 mm panel |
| **The guard ring** | both pin holes, so print `Guard-Ring-*` too and check it drops on square |
| **The corner fit** | locating lip into the cavity, lid flat on the wall top, and **two** lid screws into their bosses |

They are **cut out of the built parts by intersection, never modelled a
second time**, so a coupon cannot quietly disagree with the lid and box it is
meant to be testing. Both halves are moved by the same amount in X and Y, so
laid on each other they still line up — `images/corner_coupon-*.png` draws
them mated and exploded.

How far in the cut reaches is worked out, not typed: far enough to swallow
the switch nut, the guard ring and the corner screw boss, plus
`corner_coupon_margin`. Set `corner_coupon_w` / `corner_coupon_d` to override,
and `corner_coupon_box_h` for more of the box than the 20 mm the fit needs.

Print the lid half **face down**, same as the lid; the box half as modelled,
open side up, same as the box.

### The locating lip stops short of the plug

The lip is **cut back either side of every DEUTSCH port** — across the
flange width plus `lid_lip_port_margin` (5 mm). On revB that leaves 14.2 mm
of lip still running at each end of the `-X` wall, so the lid goes on
self-aligning; revA, with a port at both ends, keeps 21.4 mm.

This came off a real print, not out of a check, and the reason no check could
have caught it is worth writing down: **the model draws the receptacle, not
the mating plug.** `dt_body_in` and `dt_body_d` describe the part that is
bolted to the wall. The plug that clips into it, its wedge lock and the wires
sweeping up out of it are not drawn at all. On paper the lip clears the
receptacle by 4.4 mm; in the real box the plug takes that and more.

Cutting only the plug's span, rather than the whole wall run, is deliberate.
Strip the lip from a whole wall and you lose location on that axis; strip it
from two opposite walls and the lid is held by the screws alone, which the
build now warns about. `lid_lip_omit` still takes a list of walls if you ever
want the whole run gone.

Nothing else is affected: with `lid_seal` off there is no groove for the
cutback to interfere with.

### The bosses now bite into the wall

Cutting this coupon turned up a real fault. The lid screw bosses sat exactly
**tangent** to the cavity walls — touching along a line, merged with nothing,
held only by the floor. In the whole box that is invisible, because the floor
ties everything together. Cut the floor away and the bosses fell out as loose
posts.

`boss_wall_bite` (1.0 mm) now sets them that far *into* the wall, so boss and
wall genuinely fuse. The screw moves 1 mm outboard with them — to ±76 / ±46 —
which there is room for, and `checks()` proves it against the groove whenever
`lid_seal` is switched back on. The boss does not reach any further into the cavity than it
did, so nothing near the board changed.

## Flat coupon — the quick one

`exports/Test-Coupon.3mf` is a 35 mm square of the lid: same 3.5 mm thickness,
same Ø25 hole, same Ø28.2 × 0.5 recess, same 45° cone. About 4 cm³, so a few
minutes on the printer, and it answers the three things the full lid cannot
tell you until it is too late:

1. does the cone print clean face down, with no support and no sagging ring;
2. how far does the head actually stand proud **with its O-ring under it**?
   The model assumes 1.5 mm. Measure it, and if it is more, raise `guard_h`
   to keep 5 mm of guard above the button — the build checks that sum;
3. does the nut turn. 35 mm is the switch pitch, and the nut is 32.2 across
   flats but **37.2 across corners**, so on the real lid the nut corners of
   two neighbours nearly meet. The coupon is that spacing exactly.

Set `build_coupon` to `False` once it has served its purpose.

## Printing

**Everything is self-supporting. No supports on any part.**

Box: as modelled, open side up. The DEUTSCH apertures are round, the nut
pockets bridge a short span, and the floor holes are small.

Lids: **face down**, which is the reason for the cone above and gives the
better front surface. The M3 countersinks are 90° included, so they land at
exactly 45° too. The locating lip ends up on the upper side, where it is not
an overhang.

Guard ring: **bond face up** — guard end on the bed, the two pins printed
last. This is the one part whose orientation is the opposite of the lid's,
and it is why it is a separate part at all.

Everything fits an Ender 5 bed.
