# rcm enclosure

A sealed, 3D-printable housing for the rcm board: an open-top tray plus a lid.
Two lids are generated — the **keypad** face carrying eight 25 mm anti-vandal
switches, and a **blank** plate that is the starting point for the
relay-control lid (whose I/O leaves through the lid rather than the box).

Everything is generated by one script. There is nothing hand-modelled, so any
dimension can be changed and the parts rebuilt in about a minute.

```
"C:/Program Files/FreeCAD 1.1/bin/freecadcmd.exe" build_enclosure.py
"C:/Program Files/FreeCAD 1.1/bin/freecadcmd.exe" render_views.py     # review images
```

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `build_enclosure.py` | the model. `PARAMS` at the top is the only block to edit |
| `render_views.py` | shaded views, sections and the bezel print profile into `images/`, no GUI needed |
| `Enclosure-Box.FCStd`, `Lid-*.FCStd` | **generated — never edit these by hand**, they are overwritten every build |
| `exports/*.step`, `exports/*.stl` | for the slicer / for other CAD |

## Sizes

| | mm |
|---|---|
| Outside | 168 × 98 × 52 (box 46 + lid 6) |
| Cavity | 158 × 88 × 43 deep |
| Wall / floor | 5 / 3 |
| PCB | 140 × 70, on 5 mm standoffs, 9 mm clear to the walls all round |

The box is bigger than the board by more than it first looks it should be. The
9 mm gap is not padding: it is what lets a Ø8 lid-screw boss stand in each
cavity corner without touching the board, and the 5 mm wall is what leaves a
1.3 mm sealing land each side of the seal groove. Shrink either and the seal or
the screws have to go somewhere else.

## Sealing

A **face seal**: a 2.4 × 1.5 mm groove in the *lid* underside takes a 2 mm
silicone O-ring cord, and the flat top of the box wall squeezes it by ~25 %.
The groove is in the lid because the lid has material to spare around it — the
same groove cut into the wall top would leave paper-thin lands. Inboard of the
groove a 2 mm locating lip drops into the cavity, so the parts self-align
before any screw goes in and the lip acts as a second, labyrinth barrier.

Six M3 screws pull it together, into brass heat-set inserts in the bosses
(Ø4.2 holes; change `boss_hole_d` to 2.6 if you would rather self-tap). The
short walls deliberately get no mid-span screw — that is where the DEUTSCH
ports live, and a full-height boss would sit inside the connector body. That
leaves 75–80 mm between screws, which is the weakest part of the seal. If it
proves marginal in the rain, add positions to `screw_extra` and reprint the
lid; nothing else has to change.

There is **no USB window**. An open hole in the wall would undo the seal, and
the USB port is a service port — take the lid off to reach it.

## Mounting it to the car

Provisional and deliberately dumb: four Ø4.5 holes straight through the floor
at (±55, ±39.5), for M4. They sit in the 9 mm border strip that runs outside
the PCB, so the heads are reachable with a long ball-end key **without lifting
the board out**, and nothing on the board is above them.

Each one is a hole through the sealed floor, so **use a bonded seal ("dowty")
washer under each head**. A button head sits proud in the strip and fouls
nothing, which is why there is no counterbore; set `floor_hole_cbore_d` if you
want one. Move or add positions in `floor_holes` — the build checks each one
against the PCB footprint, the walls and the screw bosses.

This is a placeholder for a real bracket once the seating position is settled
and it is known where the box can actually live.

## Wire entry — DEUTSCH DT, one footprint for all three sizes

Power and CAN come in through DT flange receptacles ("DT-F") in the end walls,
not through slots. This is what makes the sealing claim honest and it keeps
the board's own screw terminals inside, on short pigtails.

The three sizes are interchangeable in this housing, which is worth knowing
because it means one printed part accepts whichever you have in the drawer.
From the TE customer drawings, all of **DT04-2P / -3P / -4P -L012** share the
flange (40.51 × 31.75 × 3.18) and the same four mounting holes on
**30.35 × 22.45** centres — but their recommended cutouts differ:

| | recommended cutout |
|---|---|
| 2 way | 18.42 × 18.29, R5.08 |
| 3 way | round, Ø25.4 |
| 4 way | 22.10 × 20.98, R6.35 |

A **round Ø25.4** hole contains the 4-way's rounded rectangle with 0.09 mm to
spare, so the model cuts one round aperture (Ø25.8 with fit clearance) and all
three drop in. The flange covers it in every case.

Each port also gets:

- a 3 mm pad on the inside face, so the wall is locally 8 mm — stiffer, and it
  gives the nut pockets a solid floor;
- four Ø4.4 clearance holes (M4, or #8) with hex nut pockets on the inside, so
  the screws have something to bite without loose hardware. Set
  `dt_nut_pockets` to `False` if you would rather use your own nuts and
  washers. Screws need to be ~14 mm to cross the 8 mm wall.

Ports are placed by the `dt_ports` list — wall, position along it, and height.
The default puts power on one end and CAN on the other. Both sit at z = 28,
which keeps the connector body clear of the board: it reaches 22 mm inboard,
overhanging the end 13 mm of the PCB, and **both end strips of the board were
checked against `rcm.kicad_pcb` and hold nothing but SMD parts** (the USB-C
shell at ~3.2 mm is the tallest). That fact is recorded as `pcb_clear_strip`
and the build warns if a port is moved somewhere it stops being true.

TE sell a gasket that goes between the flange and the panel. Use it.

## The one number to go and measure

`pcb_tall_comp_h` = 12 mm, the tallest thing standing on the board — assumed
to be the Waveshare buck module on its header pins. **It sets the height of
the whole box on its own**, because at 35 mm switch pitch the switches sit
directly over the buck, so the switch tail and the component stack:

```
inner height = standoff 5 + PCB 1.6 + component 12 + switch tail 23.8 + air 2 = 45
```

Measure the real assembly and put the number in. Every millimetre off the
component height is a millimetre off the box.

## The switches

The switch numbers are not new work — they are lifted from
`G:\My Drive\CAD\Exocet\Keypad\Keypad-Face.FCStd`, which was printed and
fitted and worked: Ø25.0 through hole, Ø28.0 × 2.0 deep bezel recess so the
head sits flush, 35 mm pitch, 2 rows of 4. Do not change them without a test
print.

What did change is how the recess ends. A square shoulder from Ø28 down to
Ø25 leaves a **1.5 mm flat annular ceiling** — printed face down, that is a
horizontal overhang and the one feature on either part that needs support. It
is now a **45° cone** instead: the recess stays full Ø28 for its whole 2 mm,
so the head still drops in flush, and then the bore closes to Ø25 over the
next 1.5 mm at exactly the angle a slicer will bridge unaided. See
`images/bezel_detail.png`, which draws the profile the way it prints.

`sw_bezel_transition` also takes `"radius"` (a quarter-round — softer to look
at, but it runs tangent to the seat, so a narrow ring of it ends up near
horizontal and may want support after all) and `"step"` for the original.

The lid is **6 mm**, not 4. The cone needs 1.5 mm of depth to run into, and at
4 mm that left only 0.5 mm of straight bore under it. At 6 mm there is 2.5 mm,
the lid is stiffer where the seal needs it, and there is still 6.3 mm of
switch thread for the nut. Going thicker eats that thread; the build prints
the figure and warns below 5 mm.

## Test coupon — print this first

`exports/Test-Coupon.stl` is a 35 mm square of the lid: same 6 mm thickness,
same Ø25 hole, same Ø28 × 2 recess, same 45° cone. About 4 cm³, so a few
minutes on the printer, and it answers the three things the full lid cannot
tell you until it is too late:

1. does the cone print clean face down, with no support and no sagging ring;
2. does the head still sit flush **with its O-ring under it** — if it stands
   proud, deepen `sw_bezel_depth` by the squashed thickness of that O-ring
   and reprint the coupon, not the lid;
3. does the nut turn. 35 mm is the switch pitch, and the nut is 32.2 across
   flats but **37.2 across corners**, so on the real lid the nut corners of
   two neighbours nearly meet. The coupon is that spacing exactly.

Set `build_coupon` to `False` once it has served its purpose.

## Printing

**Everything is self-supporting. No supports on any part.**

Box: as modelled, open side up. The DEUTSCH apertures are round, the nut
pockets bridge a short span, and the floor holes are small.

Lids: **face down**, which is the reason for the cone above and gives the
better front surface. The M3 countersinks are 90° included, so they land at
exactly 45° too. The locating lip and the seal groove both end up on the
upper side, where neither is an overhang.

Everything fits an Ender 5 bed.
